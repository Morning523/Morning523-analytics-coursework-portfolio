# Biofuel Market Entry Strategy: Should Aether Energy Enter Biofuels?

*Case competition exercise, team "CrossBridge". "Aether Energy" is a fictional oil & gas major used as the case subject; all market, regulatory and comparable-deal data below are drawn from public sources. I led the financial case (JV structuring, DCF/IRR modelling, capital affordability) and the risk assessment section; teammates led market/competitor analysis, value-chain/technical analysis, and the financing/due-diligence comparison — their names are not published here.*

## Executive summary

**Question:** should Aether enter biofuels, and how should it structure the entry?

1. **Is entry strategically necessary?** Yes — regulation, competition and investor pressure make entry effectively unavoidable: UK Net Zero 2025 legally binding targets, RTFO rising from 15.7% to 20% by 2030, the SAF mandate rising from 3.6% to 10%, competitors (BP, Phillips 66, EET) already investing, and investor ESG pressure to diversify.
2. **Does Aether have the capability to scale?** Yes — brownfield refinery assets (in-kind value ~$80–120M), capital agility ($180M cash for 50% ownership of the JV), transferable chemical-processing know-how, and an existing distribution/off-take network give Aether a structural advantage over a from-scratch entrant.
3. **What entry model maximises value?** A two-track structure: a 50/50 joint venture for processing (sharing capex and technology risk) plus organic growth in distribution (keeping 100% of blending margin, zero new capex via terminal retrofit).
4. **Does it create sufficient return?** Yes — modelled at 31.9% IRR on $180M cash equity, with manageable downside risk through hedging, alternative sourcing, and off-take agreements.

## 1. Why biofuels, why now

The UK biofuel market is shifting toward renewable diesel (HVO) and sustainable aviation fuel (SAF) — both "drop-in ready" (100% compatible with existing infrastructure, no blending-ratio limits) and offering up to 70–100% carbon savings, versus bioethanol and biodiesel's shorter shelf life, storage sensitivity, and lower carbon savings (15–60%).

Two structural forces are converging: oil & gas majors are leveraging existing fossil-fuel infrastructure and profits to fund biofuel production, while government-backed innovation hubs push next-generation technology to close the 2030 supply gap. The recommendation is for Aether to prioritise HVO in the UK market first, with SAF and European expansion as the natural second step.

## 2. Why Aether can win: structural cost advantage

Converting an existing brownfield O&G site is materially cheaper than building new:

- **Aether (brownfield conversion):** $1,200/kt
- **Greenfield (new build):** $1,600+/kt — roughly 25%+ more expensive
- Reference points: Eni Gela $617/kt and La Mède $600/kt (large brownfield, 500kt+); Neste $1,460/kt and DGD $2,100/kt (greenfield)

The in-kind contribution from Aether's existing site (hydrogen/SMR plant, utilities, convertible hydrocracker, and an existing UK distribution channel/customer base) is valued at ~$80–120M, reducing the cash investment required relative to a new entrant.

**Capital affordability:** Aether's ~$3.8bn EBITDA converts to ~$0.9bn net income and roughly $1bn/year of cash generation. The proposed $180M investment represents under 20% of one year's annual cash — a low-strain commitment relative to balance-sheet capacity.

## 3. Entry model: JV processing + organic distribution

**Processing (joint venture):** Aether partners with an established biofuel technology leader (modelled on Eni's Ecofining™ technology) for a 300kt/yr HVO/SAF facility, 50/50 equity, phased at $90M in Year 2 and $90M in Year 3. The partner brings deep RED II / low-carbon fuel compliance expertise and an existing JV track record; Aether brings the brownfield site and UK distribution reach.

**Distribution (organic):** a zero-capex terminal blending retrofit, using precision in-line blending skids with real-time ASTM-compliant dosing, retrofitted onto Aether's existing terminals. Blended product is sold 100% through Aether's own margin (not shared with the JV) via its existing B2B fleet and retail channels, qualifying for RTFC double-counting (UCO-based HVO earns 2x certificates) and SAF Mandate premiums (£4.70/L buy-out floor).

**Why this beats the alternatives:** a benefit/risk comparison across five entry types at each value-chain stage —

| Entry type | Where it wins | Where it loses |
|---|---|---|
| Outsourcing | No capital required; hedges single feedstock source | No European refiner offers third-party HVO toll-processing — no execution pathway exists |
| Organic growth (distribution only) | Builds customer relationships before committing upstream capital; guaranteed buyer base under RED III/RTFO | Without a production base, margins are limited to trading spread |
| **JV / partnership (processing)** | De-risks CAPEX at scale (benchmarked: VARO–Gunvor $600M/350ktpa, Cepsa–Bio-Oils €1.2B/500ktpy, Galp–Mitsui €400M/270ktpa); partner brings technology, permits, hydrogen supply | Governance complexity can slow operational decisions; partner alignment required on green vs. grey hydrogen |
| M&A | Acquires operating teams, permits and technology immediately, eliminating a 3–5 year build timeline | Almost no standalone HVO processing assets are on the market; valuation premiums reflect strategic scarcity |
| Greenfield build | Full margin capture, no partner profit-sharing, optimal plant design from scratch | 3–5 year build timeline; comparable projects (OMV Petrom €750M/250ktpy, Shell's cancelled $1.4B Rotterdam project) show the capex and execution risk at this scale |

## 4. Financial case

**Phase 1 (Years 1–2, Import & Validate):** sign the JV and import HVO from the technology partner to blend at existing Aether depots; sell via Aether's retail network and B2B fleet contracts; capture early RTFC margin (~$300/mt); sign a pilot SAF off-take under the SAF Mandate. This phase validates demand (~$38M) before committing Phase 2 capex.

**Phase 2 (Years 3–5, Convert & Scale):** execute the 50/50 JV to convert the O&G site into a 300kt biorefinery; ramp to 80% utilisation by Year 6 (240kt production); capture dual margin — JV processing (50% share) plus 100% of distribution blending margin.

**Unit economics:** JV margin $506/mt → Aether's 50% share = $253/mt; distribution blending adds +$180/mt (100% to Aether) → total $433/mt to Aether.

**Off-take structure at Year 5:** 51% B2B fleet (102kt, e.g. logistics/transport operators, 3–5yr volume-committed contracts), 26% retail (52kt, Aether own-brand plus wholesale, RTFC-indexed), 23% SAF airlines (46kt, 5–10yr take-or-pay under the SAF Mandate floor price).

**Returns:** Year 5 EBITDA $86.6M, rising to $103.9M at Year 6+ steady state (JV $60.7M + distribution $43.2M); Year 10 IRR 31.9%, Year 10 NPV $113.1M; payback in Year 6 with $38.5M cumulative free cash flow at that point, rising to $348M by Year 10. Annual JV capex of $90M against ~$180M total investment represents roughly 4.7% of one year's EBITDA — framed as minimal capital strain against Aether's balance sheet.

*Key modelling assumptions: HVO wholesale $2,000/mt, SAF wholesale $2,250/mt, RTFC $300/mt (conservative), process yield 88% (industry standard 85–90%), utilisation 80% (industry average 75–85%), HVO/SAF product mix 70%/30%, 12% discount rate (biofuel risk-adjusted WACC), no price escalation assumed (conservative). Total investment benchmarked at $360M (Aether cash equity $180M + in-kind site contribution valued at $100M), against a comparable Enilive-style JV structure.*

## 5. Risk assessment

| Risk | Priority | Level | Why | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Stakeholder buy-in | 1 | High | Locks up $180M for 6 years and relies on government-set prices; board or partner may reject | Model and stress-test the worst-case scenario before proposing |
| Feedstock cost volatility | 2 | High | Tariff threats and sharp crop-price swings | Diversify sourcing routes (e.g. additional UCO corridors); hedge with call options and futures |
| Regulatory dependency | 3 | High | Pricing model assumes continuation of UK environmental certificate schemes and government green policy | Pursue long-term fixed-price RTFC agreements; develop alternative sales channels (EU, other regions) post-launch |
| Exit strategy | 4 | Moderate→High | Long payback (Year 6); exposure to disruptive market events | Negotiate put/call options with the JV partner; secure Year 5–10 cash flows via off-take agreements |
| JV governance | 5 | Moderate→High | 50/50 structure risks deadlock on strategic decisions; partner may prioritise its own plants during feedstock shortages | Add an independent director to break deadlocks; contractually guarantee supply |
| Technology obsolescence | 6 | Moderate | Reliance on a volatile feedstock market; plant may be technologically outdated within 10 years | Design for future upgrades to process cheaper feedstocks; require ongoing equipment updates from the partner |

## Carbon case

HVO delivers 80–90% lower lifecycle greenhouse-gas emissions than fossil diesel when produced from waste/residue feedstocks (target reduction ≥70% against the lifecycle KPI, versus a CORSIA requirement of ≥10% improvement over fossil Jet A for SAF specifically). Combined with the JV (de-risking capex) and organic distribution growth (full margin capture via blending), the strategy is framed as both financially viable and aligned with Aether's decarbonisation targets.
