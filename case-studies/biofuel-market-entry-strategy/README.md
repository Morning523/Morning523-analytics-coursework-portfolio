# Biofuel Market Entry Strategy (Case Competition)

## Brief

A case competition brief: should a fictional oil & gas major, "Aether Energy", enter the biofuels market, and if so, how should it structure the entry? Built on public market, regulatory, and company-filing data for a case-competition scenario — not a real client engagement.

## Team

Five-person case competition team, under the name "CrossBridge". I led market analysis, competitor benchmarking and market-sizing, and contributed to shaping the market-entry direction; teammates led the financial modelling (JV structuring, DCF/IRR), risk assessment, value-chain/technical analysis, and the financing/due-diligence comparison. Teammates' names are not published here out of respect for their privacy; only my own contribution is attributed.

## Approach

1. **Strategic necessity** — regulatory mandates (UK Net Zero 2025, RTFO rising to 20% by 2030, SAF mandate rising to 10%) plus competitor moves (BP, Phillips 66, EET already investing) made entry effectively unavoidable.
2. **Capability fit** — Aether's brownfield refinery assets, existing distribution, and stable cash profile (~$1bn/year) gave it a structural cost advantage (~25% below a greenfield build) over a from-scratch entrant.
3. **Entry model** — a two-track structure: a 50/50 joint venture with an established HVO/SAF technology partner for processing (de-risking CAPEX), plus organic distribution through Aether's own retail/B2B network (capturing 100% of blending margin).
4. **Return profile** — a phased $180M cash equity commitment (plus an in-kind refinery-site contribution) modelled to 31.9% IRR, $113.1M NPV over 10 years, with payback in year 6.
5. **Risk assessment and financing options** — a prioritised risk register (stakeholder buy-in, feedstock cost volatility, regulatory dependency, exit strategy, JV governance, technology obsolescence) and a comparison of three development-finance channels (EBRD, World Bank/IFC, EIB) against collateral requirements, comparable deal terms, and prioritisation.

## Key output

- Recommended entry model: JV processing + organic distribution, not an outright acquisition or pure outsourcing — balancing capital intensity against margin capture and execution speed.
- 31.9% IRR / $113.1M NPV / payback in year 6, benchmarked against comparable industry JV and conversion deals (Enilive–PBF Energy, VARO–Gunvor, Cepsa–Bio-Oils, Galp–Mitsui).
- A benefit-cost comparison across five entry types (outsourcing, organic growth, JV/partnership, M&A, greenfield build) at each value-chain stage, with sourced rationale for why JV + organic beat the alternatives for this specific starting position.
